Privacy Policy

Shopping List — web app at shopping-list.evgenii-efimov.ru and Android app ru.evgenii_efimov.shopping_list.

Last updated: 11 August 2026.

Shopping List is made and run by one person, Evgenii Efimov, as a personal project. It has no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party SDKs beyond the Google sign-in described below. Nothing collected here is sold, rented or shared with anyone for their own purposes.

What is collected

From your Google account, when you sign in:

  • your email address;
  • your display name;
  • the URL of your Google profile picture;
  • the account identifier Google issues for you in this app (a Firebase user id). It is not your Google password — the app never sees that.

What you put into the app:

  • the titles of your lists;
  • the items on them, their quantities, and which ones are ticked off;
  • the order of lists and items, and when each was created and last changed.

There is no other collection: no location, no contacts, no photos, no device identifiers, no usage analytics, no crash reporting.

Why

Solely to run the app: to sign you in, to keep your lists attached to your account, and to show them back to you on every device you sign in from. There is no other use — no profiling, no advertising, no recommendations, no training of anything.

Encryption of list contents

Any list can be locked. Locking one encrypts the item names and quantities on that list in your browser, with a key that is unwrapped by a passkey on your device. The server stores those items only as ciphertext and holds no copy of the key, so their contents cannot be read from the server, its backups or its database.

What locking does not cover, stated plainly:

List titles stay unencrypted, including the titles of locked lists, as does the fact that a list is locked, how many items it holds, their order and their timestamps. So the secret must be in the items, not in the name of the list.

A copy of your lists is also kept unencrypted in your browser's local storage so the app works offline. Anyone holding your unlocked device can read it.

Because the server has no copy of your key, losing every registered passkey means the contents of your locked lists cannot be recovered by anyone, including the developer.

Who else handles it

  • Google — sign-in runs through Google Sign-In and Firebase Authentication, so Google handles the authentication itself and holds the account record it issues to this app. It is governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • The server — everything else is stored on a virtual private server rented and administered by the developer. The hosting provider has custody of the machine in the ordinary way any hosting provider does.

Data is never transferred to anyone else, except where the law requires it. All traffic between the app and the server is encrypted with HTTPS.

How long

Your account and your lists are kept until you delete them or ask for the account to be deleted. Deleting a list deletes its items from the database immediately. If a copy of the database taken for maintenance still holds any of it, that copy is deleted within 30 days.

Deleting your account and your data

You can delete any list, at any time, from inside the app. You can also delete the whole account from inside the app: the ⋮ menu on the lists screen → Delete account. That removes, immediately and without a queue or a waiting period, the Google account details listed above, every list, every item, and the wrapped encryption keys — and it signs the Google account itself out of this app for good. It cannot be undone, and the contents of locked lists cannot be recovered by anyone afterwards.

If you would rather ask than press the button — or you no longer have access to the app — email gpilligrim@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, and the account will be deleted within 30 days. Nothing is retained afterwards apart from database copies expiring on the schedule above.

Children

The app is not directed at children, and nothing in it is designed for or aimed at children under 13.
